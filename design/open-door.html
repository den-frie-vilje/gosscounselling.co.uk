How I can help

Therapy, and your reasons for thinking about coming to see me, will be unique to you. Here's what I offer and what each one usually involves.

From age 11 upwards

Individual counselling

We meet one to one to explore what's challenging you. For some people that's anxiety, depression or bereavement. You might be looking to explore your relationship with pornography or sex.

Whatever brings you to counselling, you are assured of a space to talk openly about it.

Couples, throuples, poly dynamics

Relationship counselling

Relationship counselling puts the focus on the relationship itself — whatever shape it takes. You won't need to explain or justify that shape to me before we start.

Sex therapy

Psychosexual therapy

If you're experiencing sexual difficulties, psychosexual therapy could be for you: pain during sex, unreliable erections, delayed ejaculation, low or absent desire, vaginismus, orgasm difficulties, vulvodynia.

People also come to talk about porn use, intimacy with a partner or partners, fetishes, or exploring kink. None of it is news to me.

For therapists & helping professionals

Clinical supervision

I offer clinical supervision to other therapists and colleagues working in the helping professions.

Supervision isn't only about bringing your client work — it's about bringing yourself too, so we can explore that work more collaboratively.

About me

Thanks for visiting. I qualified as a counsellor in 2015, after a range of experience in support work — youth mentoring, Samaritans, and supporting people in prison.

I see myself very much as an integrative therapist: I use all of my training to support you, rather than sticking to one theory. Some people come for something specific, like a sexual difficulty, but the rest of my skills and training are in the room too.

My counselling work is varied, often with anxiety, depression and bereavement. I'm also training further as a psychosexual therapist, working with painful sex, unreliable erections and other sex-related concerns.

Questions people ask

How many sessions will I need?

There isn't necessarily a set amount of therapy you 'need' — it depends on what you're coming for and what you'd like from the sessions. We can discuss it when we start, and keep it under review.

What sort of therapy do you offer?

I see myself very much as an integrative therapist: I use all my training to support you, without sticking to one type of theory. Some people come for something specific, like a sexual issue, which would fit under psychosexual therapy — but I'd still be using all my other skills and training too.

What is psychosexual therapy?

Psychosexual therapy generally focuses on a sexual issue. That could include:

  • Unreliable erections
  • Delayed ejaculation
  • Loss of, or lack of, sexual desire
  • Vaginismus
  • Painful sex
  • Orgasm problems
  • Vulvodynia

People also see a psychosexual therapist for other reasons — porn use, intimacy with a partner or partners, fetishes, or exploring kinks.

Is there a difference between counselling, therapy and psychotherapy?

The simple answer is no. In the UK, terms such as counsellor, therapist and psychotherapist aren't protected by law, so people use them interchangeably. I usually use 'counsellor' or 'therapist'. Some membership bodies and some therapists do differentiate, but in UK law there's no distinction.

Why is therapy so expensive?

As a therapist I'm self-employed, so there's no sick pay or employee benefits. There's also the training and qualifications to pay for, and continuing professional development that never stops.

The session fee has to take account of my qualifications and what they cost, along with practical things like room hire and Zoom licences.
