Counselling

Individual counselling, from the age of eleven

We meet one to one to explore what's challenging you. For some people that's anxiety, depression or bereavement. You might be looking to explore your relationship with pornography or sex. Whatever brings you to counselling, you are assured of a space to talk openly about it.

I see myself very much as an integrative therapist. That means I use all of my training to support you rather than sticking to a single theory. Some people come for something specific, but the rest of my skills and training are in the room too.

Young people from eleven are seen individually. Parents are welcome to make the first call, and we can talk through how that works.

Relationship counselling

Relationship counselling puts the focus on the relationship itself, whether you're part of a couple, a throuple or a poly dynamic. You won't need to explain or justify the shape of your relationship before we can begin.

People come because something specific has happened, because the same argument keeps returning, or because sex or intimacy has become difficult. All of it is workable.

Psychosexual therapy

If you're experiencing sexual difficulties, psychosexual therapy — often called sex therapy — could be for you. Difficulties with sex come in many forms, and none of them are unusual to hear about.

What people bring

  • Unreliable erections
  • Delayed ejaculation
  • Loss of, or lack of, sexual desire
  • Vaginismus
  • Painful sex
  • Orgasm problems
  • Vulvodynia

People also come to talk about porn use, intimacy with a partner or partners, fetishes, or exploring kink. You will not be asked to justify any of it, and nothing here is treated as a symptom of something else unless you want to look at it that way.

Training in this area

Alongside the counselling qualifications, this work is supported by a CICS Diploma in Psychosexual & Relationship Therapy (2026), a CICS Diploma in Compulsive Sexual Behaviour (2024), and continuing study towards an Advanced Diploma and an International Diploma in Clinical Sexology. Membership of COSRT, the College of Sex & Relationship Therapists, sits alongside NCPS accreditation.

Getting started

Most people ring or email first and we arrange a time to talk. You can say as little as you like at that stage — it is enough to say that it is about sex.

Clinical supervision

I offer clinical supervision to other therapists and to colleagues who work in the helping professions.

For me, supervision is not only about bringing your client work — it's about bringing yourself too, so we can explore that work more collaboratively.

Practical details

Supervision is held in person in MK3 or online, in 45-minute, one-hour or 90-minute sessions. I hold a Level 6 Diploma in Clinical Supervision (2022) and am an NCPS Accredited Member, so hours count towards professional requirements.

If your caseload includes sexual behaviour, harmful sexual behaviour or risk, that is territory with specific training behind it here: work with people convicted or accused of sexual offending (2019), the COFRA 121 compassion-focused risk-aware programme (2021), and AIM3 assessment of adolescents and harmful sexual behaviour (2024).

Frequently asked questions

How many sessions will I need?

There isn't necessarily a set amount of therapy you 'need' — it depends on what you're coming for and what you'd like from the sessions. We can discuss it when we start, and keep it under review.

What sort of therapy do you offer?

I see myself very much as an integrative therapist: I use all my training to support you, without sticking to one type of theory. Some people come for something specific, like a sexual issue, which would fit under psychosexual therapy — but I'd still be using all my other skills and training too.

What is psychosexual therapy?

Psychosexual therapy generally focuses on a sexual issue. That could include:

  • Unreliable erections
  • Delayed ejaculation
  • Loss of, or lack of, sexual desire
  • Vaginismus
  • Painful sex
  • Orgasm problems
  • Vulvodynia

People also see a psychosexual therapist for other reasons — porn use, intimacy with a partner or partners, fetishes, or exploring kinks.

Is there a difference between counselling, therapy and psychotherapy?

The simple answer is no. In the UK, terms such as counsellor, therapist and psychotherapist aren't protected by law, so people use them interchangeably. I usually use 'counsellor' or 'therapist'. Some membership bodies and some therapists do differentiate, but in UK law there's no distinction.

Why is therapy so expensive?

As a therapist I'm self-employed, so there's no sick pay or employee benefits. There's also the training and qualifications to pay for, and continuing professional development that never stops.

The session fee has to take account of my qualifications and what they cost, along with practical things like room hire and Zoom licences.
