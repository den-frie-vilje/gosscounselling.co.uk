## The Facebook cover photograph (the trees)

Saved to `docs/source-candidates/fb-cover-trees.jpg` with a sidecar. Ole asked whether it would work,
darkened, behind the hero.

**Recommendation: no. It is too small, by a wide margin, and the licence question is unresolved.**

- **Largest resolution obtainable from a public URL: 720 × 960 px.** That is the ceiling, not a
  sampling artefact. Facebook's CDN URLs are signed (`oh=`/`oe=`), and the signature covers the size
  parameters, so the rendition served to a logged-out visitor is the only one available: four
  attempts at larger variants (`cstp=mx2048x2048`, `p2048x2048`, parameters stripped) all returned
  **HTTP 403**. A hero wants 1600 px wide at minimum and ideally 2400. This is 720 px wide, so it
  would need a **2.22× upscale to reach 1600 px, or 3.33× to reach 2400 px**. That is not a
  recoverable shortfall.
- **Recompression is genuinely not the problem.** 166,361 bytes over 691,200 pixels is
  **0.241 bytes/px (1.93 bits/px)**, and the JPEG quantisation tables are fine-grained (means 15.8
  and 13.3, first coefficients `[5, 6, 11, 11, …]`) — roughly quality 85–90. The file is a
  well-encoded small image, not a mangled large one. Darkening it and putting text over it would
  hide what few artefacts there are. The pixel count alone is what disqualifies it.
- **Aspect ratio 0.750 — portrait 3:4, 720 × 960.** This is the deeper problem. It is not a cover
  photo shape at all; it is a phone snap in portrait. Cropping 16:9 from it yields **720 × 405 and
  discards 58% of the height**, which takes out the canopy and the foreground root detail and leaves
  a thin band of trunk and grass. The composition is also centre-weighted, with the big chestnut
  trunk hard right and a lens flare bottom-left — a figure standing to one side would either cover
  the trunk (the only real subject) or sit in the flare. It does not survive a wide crop.
- **Provenance: unresolved, and I could not resolve it.** It reads like his own phone photograph —
  handheld portrait framing, sun flare, casual composition, English spring woodland consistent with
  Milton Keynes — but *reads like* is not evidence. **All EXIF has been stripped** (Facebook strips
  it on upload), so there is no camera, date or authorship metadata to appeal to. I could not
  establish that it is his, and I could not establish that it is not. It may equally be a stock
  image or someone else's photograph reposted.

  **This is a licence decision for Ole and John, not something to infer.** This repo has already been
  burned once by assuming the previous designer's stock library was licensed to John when it was
  not, which is why the site currently carries no stock photography. The only safe route is to ask
  John: *did you take this photograph?* If yes, ask him for the original off his phone — which will
  be 3024 × 4032 or similar, solving the resolution problem outright, though not the portrait
  aspect. If he did not take it, it cannot be used at any size.

The file has been left in `docs/source-candidates/` only. It is not in `static/img/` and is not
wired into anything.
