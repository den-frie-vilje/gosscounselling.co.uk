# Registers and audiences

The three directions differ in look. They should differ in **voice** too, so John is choosing how
he wants to sound as well as how he wants to appear — and so each one can be judged on whether the
people he actually works with would feel spoken to.

Every factual claim stays identical across all three: the same fees, the same qualifications, the
same services, the same phone number. What changes is what gets said first, how much is explained,
and who the page is visibly addressing.

---

## Who is actually arriving

From his own site and the way he describes the work:

| Audience | What they arrive with | What would make them feel seen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Individual clients** (11+) | anxiety, depression, bereavement; often first-time therapy | that it is ordinary to not know how to start |
| **Psychosexual clients** | erections, vaginismus, painful sex, desire, orgasm, vulvodynia | the words printed plainly, so they don't have to say them first |
| **Porn / compulsive sexual behaviour** | shame, secrecy, often reading late and privately | no moralising, no framing of it as an addiction to be cured |
| **Couples, throuples, poly** | relationship structures they are tired of explaining | the shape of their relationship named on the page, not implied |
| **LGBTQ+ and kink** | fear of being the therapist's first, or of being a case study | evidence he is already at ease, without a badge |
| **Parents of 11–17s** | worry, and uncertainty whether he sees young people | the age range stated early and plainly |
| **People on low incomes** | fear the fee ends the conversation | the offer visible before the price, and framed as ordinary |
| **Therapists seeking supervision** | a professional peer decision, not a vulnerable one | a different register entirely — colleague to colleague |

Two of these are worth stating plainly because they shape everything: a lot of this traffic is
**private, late, and ashamed**, and a lot of it is **people checking whether they will have to
explain themselves before they can begin**.

## The three registers

### Quiet Practice — *discreet*

Speaks to the person who is embarrassed to be looking at all. Short sentences, no exclamation, no
sell. Assumes the reader is an adult who has already thought about this for a while. The
psychosexual work is handled without a flicker — the plainness is the reassurance. Nothing on the
page performs warmth; it demonstrates it by being unhurried and by not asking for anything.

Signature move: naming the hesitation itself, once, and then leaving it alone.

### Clear Water — *straight answers*

Speaks to the person comparing three therapists in a browser at once, and to therapists choosing a
supervisor. Answers the practical questions before they are asked — what it costs, how long, where,
online or in person, what happens after you ring. Confident and specific rather than warm. This is
the register in which the supervision offer reads best, so supervision gets more room here and is
written colleague to colleague.

Signature move: a "what happens when you get in touch" sequence, so there are no unknowns.

### Full Colour — *you can bring anything*

Speaks to the people most likely to expect a flinch: kink, porn use, poly and throuple dynamics,
LGBTQ+, and young people. Names them on the page rather than implying them, and says outright what
he already understands so nobody has to start with an explanation. Warm and direct, still plain
about sex.

Signature move: an explicit "you won't have to explain" list, and an age line so parents know.

## What must not change between them

- Fees, session lengths, qualifications, membership bodies, contact details.
- The low-income offer, and its framing as an ordinary thing to ask about.
- The plain vocabulary for sexual difficulties. No euphemism in any register.
- "Couples, throuples or poly dynamics" — his own wording, kept verbatim everywhere.
- No claims about confidentiality policy, waiting times, outcomes or approach beyond what he has
  written himself. Where a register needs to reassure, it does so by describing what will be
  discussed, not by promising a result.
